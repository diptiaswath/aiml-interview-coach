# Guide: The Offer Negotiation Round

Negotiation is the last conversation of the process, and it is a normal, expected
one. You're handed an offer and asked, implicitly, to show you can advocate for
your value with evidence and stay collaborative.

**What great looks like**
1. **Research first** — know the market range for your role, level, location, and
   company type before you talk numbers. Use several sources, not one.
2. **Know the whole package** — base, equity (vesting schedule, refreshers),
   bonus (target vs guaranteed), sign-on, and benefits. Compare total compensation,
   not just base.
3. **Mind the timing** — get the offer in writing, thank them, and ask for time to
   review. Negotiate after an offer exists, not before; let them name a number
   first when you can.
4. **Anchor with a reason** — state a specific target tied to market data and your
   impact (models shipped, systems owned), not to personal needs.
5. **Use competing offers honestly** — share real offers and deadlines, never
   invented ones. Ask for help closing the gap rather than issuing an ultimatum.
6. **Negotiate beyond money** — level and title, scope, team and project, compute
   or research access, start date, and remote or relocation terms.

**Sample phrasing:** "I'm excited about this role. Based on my research and the
scope we discussed, I was hoping for a base closer to X. Is there flexibility
there?" / "Could we revisit the level, given the ownership of the model platform
described?"

**Anti-patterns:** accepting on the spot; negotiating by email tone alone with no
call; lying about other offers; focusing only on base; sharing your current salary
when you don't have to; making demands without evidence; burning goodwill with an
ultimatum.
